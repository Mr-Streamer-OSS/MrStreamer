Questions

Getting started

Does it come with channels?
No. Mr. Streamer includes no channels and sells none. You connect the subscription you already have.
What do I need?
An existing IPTV provider with Xtream Codes access (a server address, username and password). Desktop also accepts an M3U playlist link. iPhone setup Subscriptions
Will my M3U playlist work?
On desktop, yes. Live TV works straight away. For movies and series, map the playlist's groups in Settings. Movies and episodes need finished files, so HLS entries stay out and the app says why. iPhone does not support M3U in its first release. Mapping playlists
Can I use more than one subscription?
Yes. Add as many as you like. Their channels, movies and series show together.

Watching

Can I download?
Yes. Download a movie or an episode and watch it offline on iPhone or desktop. Desktop also supports mapped playlists and whole-series downloads from 0.0.12. Saved copies remain playable after the subscription ends. Downloads
Can it record live TV?
No. Live TV can't be recorded. iPhone has no catch-up in its first release. On desktop, from 0.0.13, catch-up plays a programme that aired from its start, when your provider keeps it: with Xtream Codes, or a playlist whose catch-up addresses name the programme's start. The guide
Is there a programme guide?
Yes. iPhone shows a Guide, now/next and daily schedules when your provider supplies them. On desktop, your provider or playlist supplies a list or, from 0.0.13, a grid by time, and you can add XMLTV guides of your own, which fill in the channels it doesn't cover. Guides
Can I play on my TV?
On a Mac, with AirPlay. On Windows, with Google Cast. On Linux it plays on the computer only. iPhone has no Google Cast in its first release. What plays

Installing and updates

Which systems does it run on?
iPhone on iOS 16.4 or later: App Store for iPhone. iPad and Apple TV are outside this release. Desktop: macOS 13 or later on Apple silicon, 64-bit Windows 11 and 64-bit Linux. Download
Store or .exe on Windows?
The Store copy is signed and the Store updates it. The .exe does the same job without the Store and updates itself.
How do updates work?
iPhone updates come through the App Store. Desktop direct downloads and Homebrew copies check for updates in the app. You choose when to download and restart, and pick Stable or Nightly in Settings. The Microsoft Store manages its own updates. For the AUR, quit the app and update with makepkg or an AUR helper. Quit before replacing the app with Homebrew too. Updates

Price and privacy

Is it really free?
Yes. GPL-3.0, no ads, no purchases, no Mr. Streamer account. Your own provider is required. Don't like it? Fork it.
What does it collect?
On desktop, usage and reliability statistics are on by default: which parts of the app you use, when live TV or a movie or episode starts and stops playing and for how long, and whether playback, downloads and update checks work. Turn them off in Settings > Privacy. They never include what you watch, your searches, your provider or your login. On iPhone, statistics stay off until you choose to share them, except when an upgrade retains an earlier deliberate choice. Change the choice in Settings > Privacy. From desktop 0.0.16, usage is sent in five-minute groups and each play is reported once when it ends. Errors remain direct; after an unexpected exit, the next launch can report the last saved playback duration. From desktop 0.0.16, playback failures can also report fixed failure stages, reasons, container and codec families, delivery and fallback categories. Unknown facts stay unknown. No raw errors or stream addresses. Privacy policy
Does the website count visits?
Yes, unless your browser asks sites not to track. It counts the pages you read, the download links you use and the kind of site you came from, such as a search engine or GitHub, with no cookie, no identifier and nothing that follows you into the app. Turn it off with Site analytics at the foot of any page.
