# Desktop recording controls and baseline

## Sub-features

Record now from Live TV's now strip: a popover with the programme and channel, stop choices (**When it ends**, two minutes after the guide's end, only while the guide lists one; **In 1 hour**; **In 2 hours**; **At a time…**, a clock time today or tomorrow within 12 hours), how many of the subscription's connections are in use, Cancel and **Start recording**. When the channel plays here as MPEG-TS or clear HLS (for a multivariant playlist, in a variant of at most 1080 lines with its sound in its segments and no subtitle rendition), the popover says the recording shares Watch's connection (`recordings.connections` with `channel` answers `shares`), and Start records from Watch's own provider request: no new request, Watch plays on, and stopping either leaves the other reading it (`main/playback/live-capture.ts`). When every connection is held it says **Every connection is in use.**; when only playing another channel, or HLS that can't be shared, here holds them it offers **Stop playing and record**, which stops the preview and starts with `stopPlaying`. While the channel records the strip's button reads **Recording** and opens a popover (`dialog` named Recording) with the title, "{channel} · to {time}", **Recordings** and **Stop recording**, which calls `recordings.stop` in place so Watch plays on, and while the player holds one HLS quality for the recording (`playback.fixed`, the player store's `fixed`) the line **Quality stays fixed while this records.** under the channel line; focus returns to Record once it reads Record, also when a pointer opened the popover while the keys were elsewhere, as on Settings, Escape gives them back to Recording, and a refused Stop says why in place (`apps/desktop/test/renderer/recording-popover.test.ts`). The window bar lists **Recordings** once any recording is kept. The Recordings page has **Now** and **Recorded**: title, channel and when, a bar of captured against planned minutes, the outcome word (Recording, Paused, Complete, Stopped, Partial, Missing) and why, the size, **Stop** for one that records, Play and a menu with **Delete** for an ended one, and a footer line. Under 1280 px the size joins the second line and the outcome, with its minutes, goes under the bar; schedule rows switch with them. The outcome and why wrap rather than cut off at any width: two lines fit a row, a longer one makes it taller.

## How to get to it (user POV)

Live TV, with a channel previewing: **Record** after the list/grid switch. Schedules: Live TV's **Guide** grid, a programme's details, **Record**; or Recordings' **Scheduled** rows. Recordings: the window bar's **Recordings**, or the strip's **Recording**, then **Recordings** in its popover. An ended recording plays in the title player from this computer, as a downloaded copy does.

## Driving it with Electron CDP

Run `xvfb-run -a pnpm verify:desktop recordings` after `pnpm build` (source `scripts/recordings.ts`). The fake provider allows one connection and gives the playable test channels half-hour programmes; in this scenario they send three minutes of ffmpeg-made picture and sound at real-time rate (`longStreams`), so Watch and a recording run as long as the scenario needs. The scenario plays TEST | H.264 + AAC from Search, opens Live TV at 1280 × 800, opens Record and requires **Shares the connection playing here.** (`record-popover`), starts, and requires the recording with focus on **Recording**, Watch's clock moving on two seconds and no new provider request (`live-recording`). It opens Recordings, which stops the preview, and requires the recording to grow for 8 seconds on the one open stream (`recordings-now`); back in Live TV, Watch plays again with no new request; **Recording** opens its popover (`recording-popover`), Tab, Tab and Enter press **Stop recording**, focus lands on Record, and Watch plays on that stream (`watch-after-recording`). A second recording then shares Watch's connection too, and the page is captured with both sections (`recordings-1280`, `recordings-960`), the second reached from **Recording**'s popover (`recording-popover-960`). It then plays the stopped one to 1 s and requires a decoded picture and sound (`recording-playing`), deletes it from its menu and stops the second (`recordings-end`), requiring the provider to count no open stream. **Stop playing and record** is proven by the service contracts below, not this scenario. `proof.json` holds `recordings.list` reads and the provider's stream counts.

Run `xvfb-run -a pnpm verify:desktop recordings-hls` for an HLS channel (source `recordingsHlsScenario` in `scripts/recordings.ts`). The fake HLS host serves a live media playlist, a 3-second segment every 3 seconds behind a redirect to a tokened address, as one M3U subscription with one connection. The scenario plays TEST | HLS compatible on hls.js from Search, opens Live TV, requires **Shares the connection playing here.** in Record (`hls-record-popover`), starts, requires the recording growing while Watch's clock moves on (`hls-live-recording`), stops it from **Recording** by Tab, Tab and Enter with focus back on Record and Watch playing on (`hls-watch-after-recording`), and requires that the host sent each segment once. `proof.json` holds the recordings list and the segment numbers sent. `xvfb-run -a pnpm verify:desktop recordings-hls-qualities` runs the same journey on TEST | HLS qualities, a multivariant playlist offering 1080 and 720 lines (`hls-qualities-*` screenshots): the Recording popover must say **Quality stays fixed while this records.**, and the host must send one variant's segments only from the start of the recording until that popover shows.

What is captured, and its limits: the MPEG-TS packets as the provider sends them, every program, sound, subtitle and teletext track byte for byte, into one `.ts` file (`main/recordings/capture.ts`); nothing is decoded or re-encoded, so a recording keeps whatever codecs the channel sends and plays where the title player plays that channel's codecs. An HLS channel is read through playback's clear muxed MPEG-TS subset (`playback/hls-ts-input.ts`): its highest variant up to 1080p, its segments written one after another, discontinuities kept as sent. Encrypted, fragmented MP4, byte-range and separate-audio-rendition HLS, and a stream with no MPEG-TS program table, are refused before anything is kept. Records are in the data folder's `recordings/` (`recordings.json`, `storage.json` for the folder and limits, `schedules.json`); each file is in a folder named by its id, there or in the folder the viewer chose. A new recording's record is written before its folder is made, so a crash during its start leaves one that goes on after the relaunch. A recording's folder left without its record, from a failed start whose folder couldn't be removed or an older version's crash, is removed at the next start, only while `recordings.json` is there: in the data folder any such id folder, and in a chosen folder only one holding the `.mrstreamer-recording` marker that names this profile. Other folders are left alone. A failed start whose record `recordings.json` couldn't take out leaves an empty `.mrstreamer-failed` in its folder, which stays until `recordings.json` is written without that record: a relaunch, also after a Quit, drops the record and never captures for it, also when that folder could only be read again later while its time remained. A disk that refuses the mark too, as one gone read-only, loses that, as it loses Stop's and Quit's, and if the chosen folder is absent or cannot be read, the app cannot tell whether it holds a failed-start mark. The record stays paused, even past its stop time, until the folder can be read or the viewer Stops it. This also applies to a normal recording whose mark cannot be checked. An explicit Stop or Quit while that folder remains unreadable can still list an ended recording with no captured media. Once storage access is restored, Delete can remove it; a refused folder removal keeps the row for another try.

Every provider HTTP request also has a 15-second wait for response headers, including HLS playlists and segments. Once headers arrive that timer is cleared, leaving the response body's own watchdog in control. A public-service check lets the provider accept a connection without sending headers and requires the start to fail, close its request, release capacity and leave no recording or capture folder.

A provider can take a moment to free the slot of the preview **Stop playing and record** just closed: capture asks again after 0.5 and 1.5 s when refused, as playback does.

Without a subscription saved, Connect offers Downloads and Recordings once either keeps something: Recordings then lists Recorded only, with no Scheduled or Storage link, and plays a recording over the page (`recordings-standalone.test.ts`). In the window, `quit-dialog.test.ts` proves the Quit while recording dialog's text, its focus on Keep recording, Keep recording leaving the app running and Quit.
