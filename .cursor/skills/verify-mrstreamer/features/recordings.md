# Recordings

## Sub-features

Record now from Live TV's now strip: a popover with the programme and channel, stop choices (**When it ends**, two minutes after the guide's end, only while the guide lists one; **In 1 hour**; **In 2 hours**; **At a time…**, a clock time today or tomorrow within 12 hours), how many of the subscription's connections are in use, Cancel and **Start recording**. When every connection is held it says **Every connection is in use.**; when only playing here holds them it offers **Stop playing and record**, which stops the preview and starts with `stopPlaying`. While the channel records the strip's button reads **Recording** and opens Recordings. The window bar lists **Recordings** once any recording is kept. The Recordings page has **Now** and **Recorded**: title, channel and when, a bar of captured against planned minutes, the outcome word (Recording, Complete, Stopped, Partial, Missing) and why, the size, **Stop** for one that records, Play and a menu with **Delete** for an ended one, and a footer line. Under 1024 px the size joins the second line and the outcome, with its minutes, goes under the bar.

Schedules, from a programme's details in the guide grid: **Record** opens a panel under the details with **Repeat** (**Once**, the programme's weekday, **Every day**), **Start** and **End** padding steppers (0 to 60 min, 2 early and 5 late to begin with), a line saying when it records and whether a connection is free then, or **Every connection is in use then.** with what holds it and **Schedule anyway**, and **Schedule**. Once is the guide programme at its start, whose end follows the guide; a repeat is a channel and a local time of day, whatever is on. A scheduled programme's cell has a ring, and its details read **Scheduled**, which edits it or cancels it. Recordings lists **Scheduled** between Now and Recorded: title, channel and padded times, the repeat, its next time and how it stands (a connection is free, in use and by what, waiting for one, recording, the guide lists another programme or none then), **Edit** in a popover and a menu with **Cancel schedule**. A schedule records through the same capture and admission as Record: at its padded start, or late with the rest of its time when the app was closed, the computer slept or every connection was held, and a row says it started late and why, its bar hatching what it missed. A time that passed with nothing captured is a **Missed** row with why and **Remove**. Nothing stops for a schedule, it never wakes or starts the computer, and title-based series rules are not offered.

Storage, gaps within a recording, quitting and updates while recording belong to a later card.

## How to get to it (user POV)

Live TV, with a channel previewing: **Record** after the list/grid switch. Schedules: Live TV's **Guide** grid, a programme's details, **Record**; or Recordings' **Scheduled** rows. Recordings: the window bar's **Recordings**, or the strip's **Recording**. An ended recording plays in the title player from this computer, as a downloaded copy does.

## Driving it with Electron CDP

Run `xvfb-run -a pnpm verify:desktop recordings` after `pnpm build` (source `scripts/recordings.ts`). The fake provider allows one connection and gives the playable test channels half-hour programmes. The scenario plays TEST | H.264 + AAC from Search, opens Live TV at 1280 × 800, opens Record (`record-popover`), starts and requires the busy answer with no recording made (`record-busy`), requires the provider asked for nothing more, presses **Stop playing and record** and requires it records on the one connection (`live-recording`), opens Recordings (`recordings-now`), records 8 seconds and stops it, starts a second recording, and captures the page with both sections (`recordings-1280`, `recordings-960`). It then plays the stopped one to 1 s and requires a decoded picture and sound (`recording-playing`), deletes it from its menu and stops the second (`recordings-end`). After each Stop it requires the provider to count no open stream. `proof.json` holds `recordings.list` reads and the provider's stream counts.

What is captured, and its limits: the MPEG-TS packets as the provider sends them, every program, sound, subtitle and teletext track byte for byte, into one `.ts` file (`main/recordings/capture.ts`); nothing is decoded or re-encoded, so a recording keeps whatever codecs the channel sends and plays where the title player plays that channel's codecs. An HLS channel is read through playback's clear muxed MPEG-TS subset (`playback/hls-ts-input.ts`): its highest variant up to 1080p, its segments written one after another, discontinuities kept as sent. Encrypted, fragmented MP4, byte-range and separate-audio-rendition HLS, and a stream with no MPEG-TS program table, are refused before anything is kept. Records and files are in the data folder's `recordings/` (`recordings.json`, one folder per id); a viewer-chosen folder belongs to a later card.

Service contracts are in `apps/desktop/test/recordings.test.ts`: every track of the subtitles fixture recorded to its stop time and played through `openCopy`, busy without a provider request while Watch holds the one connection, **Stop playing and record** while the provider frees the stopped stream's slot late, and after a queued download took the connection it gave up, beside Watch with two connections, stop and broken-stream partials, a subscription removed while a start reads its M3U playlist again or turns active, quit then restart, the next stream after one with no program, a name within the file system's bytes, and HLS segments with a refused separate-audio rendition.

At the service boundary, Stop also retries saving an ended recording's current status. A failed terminal save never makes a later Stop silently succeed: it reports the save error while storage remains unavailable, then persists the retained outcome when storage works again. The public-service check blocks manifest replacement after start, lets the source end, and verifies failure, released connections and a successful retry against the file.

The window offers Stop only while a row reads Recording, so this protects a click racing the automatic end. An automatic end with no Stop can leave its save failure unreported. On relaunch, an unsaved terminal outcome reads as Partial, cut short by the quit, with the file's actual size. The service retry does not add a retry control to an ended row.

Every provider HTTP request also has a 15-second wait for response headers, including HLS playlists and segments. Once headers arrive that timer is cleared, leaving the response body's own watchdog in control. A public-service check lets the provider accept a connection without sending headers and requires the start to fail, close its request, release capacity and leave no recording or capture folder.

Run `xvfb-run -a pnpm verify:desktop schedules` (source `scripts/schedules.ts`) for scheduling. It opens Record's **At a time…** (`1-record-at-a-time`), the guide grid's next programme's panel once and on its weekday with the padding stepped by keys while the grid stays put (`2-guide-once`, `2-guide-repeat`), schedules it (`2-guide-scheduled`), opens the channel below at the same time and requires the conflict naming the first schedule (`3-guide-conflict`), schedules it anyway every day, and requires both under **Scheduled** with Edit (`5-recordings-scheduled`, `5-recordings-edit`). It then writes a missed and a late row into `recordings.json` while the app is closed, as the service writes them, and captures Recordings (`5-recordings-1280`), 960 × 600 with Edit and the grid's panel (`10-…`), **Cancel schedule** from the menu (`10-recordings-cancelled-960`), a quarter larger text, and German (`lang-de-…`). Run it more than two minutes before a half hour: the next programme's padded start must not have come.

Schedule contracts are in `apps/desktop/test/recordings.test.ts` under "recording schedules", with the clock set by the test: a programme recorded from its padded start to its end and let go; waiting while Watch holds the one connection, then recording the rest marked late for it; a missed result for a time that passed while the app was closed, staying removed once removed, and the rest of the next time recorded after a wake; a programme the guide lists another show or none for, held and never recorded; a conflict previewed without saving, then the first saved recording at their shared time while the other waits; changing padding by id and cancelling, with a recording it started going on; a change made while it records taking effect from its next time; a time whose recording was stopped and deleted never recorded again; and removal with its subscription. "recording slot times" proves daylight saving in Europe/Amsterdam and a slot across midnight on its weekday only. A schedules.json this build can't read, or holding a plan outside the window's limits, is refused and left as it is, as recordings.json is.

## Gotchas

A schedule whose time comes while every connection is held asks again every 10 seconds, and when a recording ends; one whose start failed asks again about once a minute. Nothing playing or downloading stops for it. Schedules due together on one subscription start one at a time, by their start and then the order they were saved in. A programme schedule waits for its guide to be read before it starts, so it never records another show.

Schedules are kept in `schedules.json` beside `recordings.json`, which a build from before them leaves alone. Such a build refuses a `recordings.json` holding a missed result, leaving it as it is and recording nothing, as with any file it can't read; no released build predates Record now.

A provider can take a moment to free the slot of the preview **Stop playing and record** just closed: capture asks again after 0.5 and 1.5 s when refused, as playback does.

Without a subscription saved the app shows Connect and standalone Downloads only: recordings are kept but not listed until one is added again.
