# Recordings

## Sub-features

Record now from Live TV's now strip: a popover with the programme and channel, stop choices (**When it ends**, two minutes after the guide's end, only while the guide lists one; **In 1 hour**; **In 2 hours**; **At a time…**, a clock time today or tomorrow within 12 hours), how many of the subscription's connections are in use, Cancel and **Start recording**. When every connection is held it says **Every connection is in use.**; when only playing here holds them it offers **Stop playing and record**, which stops the preview and starts with `stopPlaying`. While the channel records the strip's button reads **Recording** and opens Recordings. The window bar lists **Recordings** once any recording is kept. The Recordings page has **Now** and **Recorded**: title, channel and when, a bar of captured against planned minutes, the outcome word (Recording, Paused, Complete, Stopped, Partial, Missing) and why, the size, **Stop** for one that records, Play and a menu with **Delete** for an ended one, and a footer line. Under 1024 px the size joins the second line and the outcome, with its minutes, goes under the bar.

Schedules, from a programme's details in the guide grid: **Record** opens a panel under the details with **Repeat** (**Once**, the programme's weekday or a repeat's own days, **Every day**), **Start** and **End** padding steppers (0 to 60 min, 2 early and 5 late to begin with, within the 12 hours a recording may run; a repeat of a longer programme records 12 hours), a line saying when it records and whether a connection is free then, or **Every connection is in use then.** with what holds it and **Schedule anyway**, and **Schedule**. Once is the guide programme at its start, whose end follows the guide; a repeat is a channel and a local time of day, whatever is on. A scheduled programme's cell has a ring, and its details read **Scheduled**, which edits it or cancels it. Recordings lists **Scheduled** between Now and Recorded: title, channel and padded times, the repeat, its next time and how it stands (a connection is free, in use and by what, waiting for one, recording, the guide lists another programme or none then), **Edit** in a popover and a menu with **Cancel schedule**. A schedule records through the same capture and admission as Record: at its padded start, or late with the rest of its time when the app was closed, the computer slept or every connection was held, and a row says it started late and why, its bar hatching what it missed, counted from when the schedule was saved for one saved partway through its time. With `recordings.json` unreadable, nothing is scheduled either. A time that passed with nothing captured is a **Missed** row with why and **Remove**. Nothing stops for a schedule, it never wakes or starts the computer, and title-based series rules are not offered.

Interruptions: a recording owes its time from its start to its stop. When capture stops early for the stream, the disk, the storage limits, the computer's sleep, a crash, or a system shutdown or session end, it reads **Paused** in Now with why and since when, and goes on with the rest of its time, appended to its file, once the app runs, the computer is awake, a connection is free and there is room. What passed meanwhile is a gap: its row says "20:31 to 20:45 missed." and why, its bar hatches it, and nothing fills it, from catch-up or otherwise. A gap begins where capture last wrote. Stop, on a recording or a paused one, and an explicit Quit the viewer confirmed end it for good; a stopped one never goes on. So does an HLS playlist that says its stream is over (`#EXT-X-ENDLIST`), and a provider that ends its answer cleanly within 30 seconds three times in a row, as with a short clip: both read Partial, the stream. One that reaches its stop with gaps reads Partial, its last gap closed at the stop. A live HLS channel going on after a gap shorter than its playlist's last three segments can repeat its first seconds: the card's known limit. A chosen folder that isn't there, as on a drive not plugged in, refuses a start rather than being made on another disk, and Settings' Open doesn't make it either.

Storage: Settings, **Recordings** shows the folder new recordings go to with Change and Open, **Keep free on disk** (5 GB to begin with) and **Space for recordings** (No limit to begin with). At either limit a recording pauses, and goes on once there is room; a start with no room says so and asks the provider nothing. Nothing is deleted to make room: recordings stay until the viewer deletes them. Those made before a folder change stay where they were made.

Close, Quit and updates: closing the window never asks. While a recording's time goes on or a schedule is kept, the app keeps running without its window, and launching it again opens the window. macOS keeps the app as before; Windows and Linux show a tray icon with Open and Quit while the window is closed, and Ctrl+Q quits from the window. Only an explicit Quit asks first, while a recording's time goes on: **Quit while recording?** with Quit and Keep recording. Without a window, the app quits by itself once nothing records, no schedule is kept and no end waits to be written down. A shutdown never asks and leaves recordings to go on at the next start. A ready update with a recording live reads **Update after recording** and installs once nothing records, the release last asked for, with nothing new starting from then until the app quits or the install is refused; Restart now stops the recordings first.

## How to get to it (user POV)

Live TV, with a channel previewing: **Record** after the list/grid switch. Schedules: Live TV's **Guide** grid, a programme's details, **Record**; or Recordings' **Scheduled** rows. Recordings: the window bar's **Recordings**, or the strip's **Recording**. An ended recording plays in the title player from this computer, as a downloaded copy does.

## Driving it with Electron CDP

Run `xvfb-run -a pnpm verify:desktop recordings` after `pnpm build` (source `scripts/recordings.ts`). The fake provider allows one connection and gives the playable test channels half-hour programmes. The scenario plays TEST | H.264 + AAC from Search, opens Live TV at 1280 × 800, opens Record (`record-popover`), starts and requires the busy answer with no recording made (`record-busy`), requires the provider asked for nothing more, presses **Stop playing and record** and requires it records on the one connection (`live-recording`), opens Recordings (`recordings-now`), records 8 seconds and stops it, starts a second recording, and captures the page with both sections (`recordings-1280`, `recordings-960`). It then plays the stopped one to 1 s and requires a decoded picture and sound (`recording-playing`), deletes it from its menu and stops the second (`recordings-end`). After each Stop it requires the provider to count no open stream. `proof.json` holds `recordings.list` reads and the provider's stream counts.

What is captured, and its limits: the MPEG-TS packets as the provider sends them, every program, sound, subtitle and teletext track byte for byte, into one `.ts` file (`main/recordings/capture.ts`); nothing is decoded or re-encoded, so a recording keeps whatever codecs the channel sends and plays where the title player plays that channel's codecs. An HLS channel is read through playback's clear muxed MPEG-TS subset (`playback/hls-ts-input.ts`): its highest variant up to 1080p, its segments written one after another, discontinuities kept as sent. Encrypted, fragmented MP4, byte-range and separate-audio-rendition HLS, and a stream with no MPEG-TS program table, are refused before anything is kept. Records are in the data folder's `recordings/` (`recordings.json`, `storage.json` for the folder and limits, `schedules.json`); each file is in a folder named by its id, there or in the folder the viewer chose. A recording's folder a crash left before its record was written is removed at the next start, only while `recordings.json` is there: in the data folder any such id folder, and in a chosen folder only one holding the `.mrstreamer-recording` marker that names this profile. Other folders are left alone.

Service contracts are in `apps/desktop/test/recordings.test.ts`: every track of the subtitles fixture recorded to its stop time and played through `openCopy`, busy without a provider request while Watch holds the one connection, **Stop playing and record** while the provider frees the stopped stream's slot late, and after a queued download took the connection it gave up, beside Watch with two connections, stop and broken-stream partials, a subscription removed while a start reads its M3U playlist again or turns active, quit then restart, the next stream after one with no program, a name within the file system's bytes, HLS segments with a refused separate-audio rendition, and Auto recording a channel's next stream when its playlist no longer names the first. An end that couldn't be written down is listed with **Not saved yet. Trying again.**, and the schedule check writes it every 10 seconds until storage works; Stop also retries it and reports the save error meanwhile, never silently succeeding. Until it is written, a relaunch reads it as one the app didn't see end: paused when its time remains, else Partial cut by the quit with the file's actual size. Stop, also on one that ended by itself, a subscription's removal and Restart now, all as stopped, and a confirmed Quit, as the quit, also for one whose own end wasn't written, leave an empty `.mrstreamer-stopped` or `.mrstreamer-quit` in the recording's folder, so a relaunch ends that one as Stopped or Partial, the quit, as of then, never going on. Only a disk that refuses both writes, as one gone read-only, loses that: the Quit doesn't wait for it. Those writes are tried again while `schedules.json` can't be read, too. An unreadable `storage.json` is never written over: Settings, Recordings says it can't be read, and new recordings refuse to start while paused ones go on where they began, under the default limits (5 GB kept free, no space limit).

Every provider HTTP request also has a 15-second wait for response headers, including HLS playlists and segments. Once headers arrive that timer is cleared, leaving the response body's own watchdog in control. A public-service check lets the provider accept a connection without sending headers and requires the start to fail, close its request, release capacity and leave no recording or capture folder.

## Gotchas

A schedule whose time comes while every connection is held asks again every 10 seconds, and when a recording ends; one whose start failed asks again about once a minute. Nothing playing or downloading stops for it. Schedules due together on one subscription start one at a time, by their start and then the order they were saved in. A programme schedule waits for its guide to be read before it starts, so it never records another show.

Schedules are kept in `schedules.json` beside `recordings.json`, which a build from before them leaves alone. Such a build refuses a `recordings.json` holding a missed result, leaving it as it is and recording nothing, as with any file it can't read; no released build includes Record now.

A provider can take a moment to free the slot of the preview **Stop playing and record** just closed: capture asks again after 0.5 and 1.5 s when refused, as playback does.

Without a subscription saved, Connect offers Downloads and Recordings once either keeps something: Recordings then lists Recorded only, with no Scheduled or Storage link, and plays a recording over the page (`recordings-standalone.test.ts`). In the window, `quit-dialog.test.ts` proves the Quit while recording dialog's text, its focus on Keep recording, Keep recording leaving the app running and Quit.
