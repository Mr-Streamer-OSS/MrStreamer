# iOS downloads

On iOS, a transfer that starts from zero again says **started again**. That state also covers unusable or absent resume data; it does not establish that the provider changed the file. Check through ordinary Downloads Retry with the same fixture bytes and ETag, then compare the final media hash. An uninterrupted transfer must keep its normal progress text after saving progress. Native resume requires its own native attempt and wire-range binding.

On iOS, a queue row's glass button is **Pause**, or **Resume** once paused; its long-press sheet and VoiceOver actions add `Move <name> up`/`down` among the downloads waiting their turn, off at either end, and **Cancel download**. A paused row reads **Paused** until bytes arrived, then **Paused · <bytes> of <size>** when its size is known, or **Paused · <bytes>** otherwise. It keeps its place through a relaunch and is never started by the native queue, in the background either, until resumed; the queue goes in the list's order. A pause the native side hasn't kept yet, such as a hold that timed out and whose late write failed, or one a relaunch interrupted, reads as its transfer while that goes on, with **Pause** to ask again, until a later refill, the next foreground or another Pause keeps it. `apps/ios/test/downloads-pauses.test.ts` covers pause/resume, an unkept pause, relaunch and ordering against the Linux model of the native queue (`native-downloads.ts`); `apps/ios/test/downloads-screen.test.ts` covers the row actions and labels. Native visuals and VoiceOver, the Swift owner's pause, hold and order on a phone, background wakes, OS-deferred scheduling and force quit need a native run.

On iOS, a copy is kept as `media.<listed container>`, and AVFoundation types a file on the phone by that name: an MP4 or MOV listed as `mkv`, such as the fixture's generated movies 0, 3, 6 and so on (`feature.mp4` under `mkv`), fails with `AVFoundationErrorDomain -11828` where it streams fine. `apps/ios/modules/title-formats` answers `direct` for it, playback gives it to the player as `progressive`, and `apps/ios/modules/title-formats/ios/MisnamedCopy.swift` opens it through a temporary `.mp4` link removed with the asset, only when its first box is MP4 or MOV: a copy that is neither, which the session also answers `direct`, opens under its own name and still says **Mr. Streamer can't play this file**. On the Simulator, download Earth Story 0, Arena Story 1 (the same bytes listed `mp4`) and **TEST | Two sound tracks and subtitles (MULTI)**, the supported H.264/E-AC-3 Matroska fixture `title-h264-eac3-subs.mkv`, while the provider is running. Compare the copies' first bytes and SHA-256 in the app's `Documents/downloads`, then stop the provider and put a connection listener on its port. Play each from Downloads, skip, Stop and play again: each must play and resume, and nothing may reach the provider's port. For the misnamed copy only, the app's `tmp/misnamed-copies` must hold only that play's link; after Stop it must be gone, and replay must use a new link without retaining the first. The correctly named MP4 and real Matroska controls must not create such a link, and none may remain after the last Stop. After Stop, overwrite the owned test copy's `media.mkv` with 1 MiB of zero bytes: its eight-byte prefix has no allowlisted box name. Playback must say it can't play this file, and `tmp/misnamed-copies` must stay empty. `apps/ios/test/format-playback.test.ts` covers what playback hands the player.

On iOS, the copies are listed under **On this device** and the list closes with **<size> on this device · <size> free**, the same words on iPhone, iPad and Duo; check an iPad with a completed copy, in English and one other language.

On iOS, the tab header's Downloads action keeps one 44-point slot and icon centre in every state, with no text: its circle is a still ring of the transfer's received/size, a static dotted circle when the size is unknown, a dim track while queued, waiting or paused, and the idle glyph with an exclamation badge once only retryable failures remain. Its label stays `Downloads, <status>`; Search's entry and Watch's button keep the short text under their smaller glyph. `apps/ios/test/download-status.test.ts` covers the labels, the status order, the header showing no text and its routes. On the simulator, pace a fixture title with the control port's `/title-transfers` to capture two partial fractions, pause it, queue another and break one off, comparing the action's frame across those states beside Search and Settings; ring alignment, weight against Settings, physical phones and spoken VoiceOver need their own native checks.
