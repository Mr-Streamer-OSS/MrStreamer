## Interlaced H.264 channels (desktop 0.0.24 development, #868)

An MPEG-TS channel's H.264 sequence parameter set says whether it allows field pictures (`frame_mbs_only_flag` 0); `apps/desktop/src/main/playback/inspect.ts` then names the picture `h264-interlaced`. The window's player never lists that codec (`apps/desktop/src/renderer/src/player/decoders.ts`), so `planConversion` re-encodes the picture through the shared `yadif=deint=interlaced` filter in `apps/desktop/src/main/playback/conversion-settings.ts`, one frame per frame at the source rate; the sound is copied when the player decodes it. Live MPEG-TS detection is bounded to 1 MiB of video payload within 2 MiB or 2.5 seconds of inspection; converted HLS can settle after about 32 KiB of a segment. Missing or truncated SPS retains existing conversion decisions. A frame-only SPS avoids picture conversion; whole-stream byte preservation also requires decodable sound and no other conversion request. Field-capable sequences containing progressive pictures still re-encode. The source fixture preserves 25 frames per second; 50 fields per second output is not qualified. Receivers list `h264-interlaced` in `RECEIVER_DECODERS` (`packages/contracts/src/playback.ts`), so a TV still gets the picture as sent. HLS channels hls.js plays directly, movies, episodes and recordings played as titles are not inspected this way and stay as sent.

Source coverage: `apps/desktop/test/playback.test.ts` plays the fake provider's "TEST | H.264 interlaced" (`apps/desktop/test/fixtures/h264-interlaced.mpegts`, a white bar crossing a gray picture as fields) and requires ffmpeg's `idet` to read all 75 source pictures as combed and all 75 delivered ones as progressive, at 25 frames a second with AAC copied; it fails with the inspector change reverted. "passes a stream the player decodes through unchanged" keeps progressive H.264 byte-identical. On this 128 × 72 fixture `idet` reads slow test picture (`testsrc2`, still bars) as undetermined or wrong, so compare the moving-bar channel only.

Built-app proof is still required: play "TEST | H.264 interlaced" and "TEST | H.264 + AAC" in Watch, capture paused frames of the moving bar and require straight edges on the first and an unchanged picture on the second, then seek in the retained timeline, stop and reopen, and play "TEST | Subtitles and two sound tracks" to keep sound and subtitle choices. CPU, GPU and power before and after, macOS and Windows hardware decoding, and real 1080i broadcasts remain unmeasured; don't infer any of them from the filter or the codec name.

## Google Cast on macOS and Linux (desktop 0.0.24 development, #882)

`receiverAdapters` in `apps/desktop/src/main/index.ts` offers Google Cast on Windows, and on macOS and Linux only when `MR_STREAMER_CAST=on`. Every system runs the same sender, `apps/desktop/src/main/receivers/cast/`, with no platform branch. A Mac with Cast turned on offers both: O, the title's TV button and More > Play on open Cast's list, which keeps an AirPlay row that opens Apple's list at the controls.

Qualification status is missing hardware, not an unsupported route. No physical Cast device has played from this app on any desktop system, Windows included, so macOS and Linux stay an experimental opt-in and nothing promotes them.
