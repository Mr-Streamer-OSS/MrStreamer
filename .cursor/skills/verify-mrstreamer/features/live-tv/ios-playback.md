# iOS tracks, recovery and receivers

On iOS, Watch offers **Audio** and **Subtitles** for a live channel as it does for a title, in the row under its clock that also holds **Play from start**. Each sheet reads the stream's native tracks as it opens and selects the exact record for that attempt, item and revision; its checkmarks follow readback, and Off shows only where the native group allows it. A group still loading or unreadable says "Not known yet. Try again in a moment.", and a stream with none says "No choices on this channel.". Automatic picks follow the saved policies' Live rules (`apps/ios/src/media-policy.ts`). A confirmed pick holds across automatic reconnects and fallbacks. While the channel remains the current playback choice, playing it again, as a Quality change does, keeps that pick while its login stands, rematched by language, label and name on the new stream. Stop ends the choice; replay then applies the saved automatic policies. A different channel, catch-up playback or a changed login starts with no manual picks. A sheet opened before a reconnect asks nothing of the new attempt. `apps/ios/test/playback.test.ts` ("a live channel's sound and subtitles in Watch") drives the exact pick, the Quality carry, its limits, the unknown and empty sheets and the outlived sheet through the real account, playback and Watch; `track-choices.test.ts` covers Live policy, Off gating, holds across reconnect and fallback, descriptor rematching and stale rows. Audible and visible changes on multi-track Live media, the native bridge during a track removal or replacement while a pick is pending, VoiceOver and large text in Watch, and iPad and Duo layouts need a native run.

On iOS, Automatic tries the channel's streams in core's `streamsToPlay` order, the next one while none has played. Once one played, a 15-second stall, a lost connection or a reconnect that shows nothing within 20 seconds reconnects to the stream a quality lower, when one is among the (at most three) streams Automatic tries, within the same four reconnects after 1, 2, 4 and 8 seconds. A lower stream that fails before it plays, off air too, steps lower again, or with nothing lower goes back to the one that played. Other provider errors reconnect the same stream, and a missing or undecodable stream that played fails. Automatic never steps back up: the lower stream plays until the channel is played again or Try again. A stream chosen in Quality and a programme from catch-up always reconnect as they are, and Automatic's step down is never saved. `apps/ios/test/media-policy.test.ts` and `live-recovery.test.ts` cover the policy and its path through the real account and player. On the simulator, the fixture server's `/live-link` pace and "BE | KWALITEIT 1" (HD about 2.6 Mbit/s, SD about 0.57 Mbit/s) reproduce the measured weak-connection case. This fixture caps each numbered, single-rendition segment response at its request-start rate; it does not retime active responses, cap aggregate traffic or pace the multitrack channel's picture/sound renditions. These measurements do not establish mid-segment link restoration or weak-link multitrack behavior. Real networks, a provider's HLS master playlists, which AVPlayer adapts by itself, and physical devices need a native run.

On iOS, **Play on TV** sends a channel only as HLS, LIVE: the phone's relay reads the provider, follows redirects and rewrites each playlist to token paths, refusing keys even when one appears on a later refresh. Leaving the app sends media STOP, revokes the token, returns the lease once the relay's readers closed, then stops the receiver and closes the channel; a lost TV or Wi-Fi shows "Lost <TV>." Proof: `apps/ios/test/cast.test.ts` (fake TV and relay) on Linux only; real receiver and iPhone behavior are unproven.
