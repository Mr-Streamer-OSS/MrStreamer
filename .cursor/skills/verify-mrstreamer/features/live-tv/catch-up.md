# Catch-up playback

For catch-up run `pnpm verify:desktop catch-up` (source `scripts/catchup.ts`). It opens Live TV's "TEST | Formats and failures", the rest of `H.264 + AAC`'s day, and clicks the first programme that aired. The fake panel breaks its first answer two seconds in. Pass means Watch plays it (advancing clock, picture, decoded audio) and says the programme's title and "Aired", reconnects and plays again, the fake panel recorded exactly two timeshift requests, each with that programme's start minute on its clock (`Europe/Amsterdam`) and its length in minutes, and no live stream request; at the recording's end Watch stops with **Watch**, which then plays the channel live with one stream request and no third timeshift request. Retained: the schedule, playing, ended and live-again screenshots, and `archiveRequested` beside `archiveExpected` in `proof.json`. It runs on the real clock: the fake panel's guide lists half hours from two hours back, and the guide keeps what aired, so the day Live TV reads, from its start to its end (`guide.schedule` with `from` and `until`), lists programmes that ended; shortly after midnight the guide adds the last few that aired the day before. `pnpm verify:desktop catch-up-hls` does the same from a playlist's Flussonic catch-up, connected by M3U link: its recording is an HLS playlist of four parts that names no end yet, and pass means Watch plays it with the programme's title and "Aired", the host was asked for the recording, and the first part it was asked for is `part-0`, not a later one near the playlist's end. Playlist conventions, time zones, where a recording starts (one moment for every stream of a channel), the archive's pacing and its end at the window's end (signalled clock discontinuities counting no time), whole-millisecond reopens and the typed errors are in `apps/desktop/test/catchup.test.ts`, `packages/core/test/catchup.test.ts` and `renderer/catchup.test.ts`. Real-provider archives and a TV receiver are not covered: catch-up plays on this computer only.
