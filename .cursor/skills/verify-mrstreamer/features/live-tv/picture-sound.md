# Picture and sound adjustments (desktop 0.0.24 development)

One set of adjustments for everything that plays on this computer: sound delay (0 to 2000 ms, sound after the picture only), opt-in leveling, aspect (as the stream says, 16:9, 4:3, 21:9), crop to one of those ratios, zoom (100 to 300 %) and Reset. They live in the active profile's `playbackAdjustments` preference (`packages/contracts/src/preferences.ts`) and go through `preferences.update`; a receiver plays the stream as sent.

- `apps/desktop/src/renderer/src/features/watch/PictureSound.tsx` is the panel: Sound delay (− and + in 50 ms steps), Leveling Off/On, Aspect, Crop, Zoom (25 % steps), Reset, the failure line with Retry, and on a receiver only its refusal line. A title opens it from the bar's **Picture and sound** button (pressed while anything differs from the defaults); Live TV from **More > Picture and sound** (`LiveMore.tsx`, which says "Adjusted" beside it). Up and Down move between rows, Left and Right move within each row; End reaches Reset while a change is active and no Retry follows it. A control with no room beside its label, from a long translation or large text, moves below the label and keeps to the right edge (`settingRow` in `apps/desktop/src/renderer/src/features/watch/menu-rows.tsx`, shared with the subtitle settings).
- `apps/desktop/src/renderer/src/player/adjustments.ts` holds what plays: `adjustPlayback`, `resetPlayback`, `retryPlayback`, `usePlaybackAdjustments` (adjustments, and failed with its message) and `useAdjustmentsRefusal` for a receiver. A failed save plays the saved adjustments again and keeps the failed choice for Retry.
- `apps/desktop/src/renderer/src/player/sound.ts` is the one sound chain. Both `engine.ts` (every live engine, through its shared lifecycle) and `title-engine.ts` (titles and downloads) attach their element. Default sound never touches Web Audio. The first delay or leveling routes attached elements through one `AudioContext`: media element source, delay, per-device-channel mono compressor pairs when leveling, gain. An element stays routed for the window's life, retaining AudioContext output latency at the defaults. Lifecycle pause, seek, source and track changes discard buffered processing. The native engine asks for CORS, which the loopback proxy grants, so Web Audio hears the element.
- `apps/desktop/src/renderer/src/player/picture-fit.ts` gives the picture's box, and `isAdjusted` in `packages/contracts/src/preferences.ts` whether any part differs from the defaults; `Picture.tsx` applies it only for `adjusted` pictures: Watch and the title player, both full and mini. Previews and multiview panes stay fitted.

Contract tests: `apps/desktop/test/renderer/picture-fit.test.ts` (picture geometry) and `apps/desktop/test/renderer/picture-sound.test.ts` (the panel: steps and picks saved within the limits, Reset, the failure line and Retry, the receiver line).

Built-app journey: build, run doctor, then `xvfb-run -a pnpm verify:desktop picture-sound` (source `scripts/picture-sound.ts`). Constant-rate fake live encodes (AAC direct, MP2 converted) and the fake title. It plays the direct channel as the stream comes and requires no Web Audio context. Then, in **More > Picture and sound** while the channel plays, it steps Sound delay to 200 ms, picks Leveling On and Crop 4:3, steps Zoom to 150 %, requires no control past its row's right edge, no value on two lines and no sideways scroll in the panel (`PANEL_OVERFLOW`, which can be evaluated with the panel open in any language), and requires the picture to move at once. After a restart, which records the saved `playbackAdjustments`, it plays the direct channel, the converted channel, the two-track title, a switch to its Spanish track and the downloaded copy with Watch offline. In that copy's **Picture and sound** menu it presses End and Enter to Reset, requires the picture to fill its area again and the saved adjustments to be the defaults. Each adjusted stage requires a box and central clip matching independently derived fixture geometry within a pixel and six media seconds keeping wall pace. Adjusted stages and the in-window Reset require, over CDP `WebAudio`, one realtime context with one routed element and its render capacity, plus baseLatency, outputLatency and getOutputTimestamp through Runtime.queryObjects. These fields do not establish acoustic offset. A third start after Reset requires no context again. Last, the window renders synthetic signals through the bundled `sound.ts` chain offline: measured delay against each setting, with and without leveling; peak and full-scale samples of quiet, full-scale and burst passages; channels kept from a six-channel input; render speed.

An earlier Linux Xvfb recording (12 November 2026), before the panel existed, wrote adjustments into `preferences.json` between starts. That run's delay, leveling, six-channel and render numbers belong to the old chain and do not qualify the revised per-channel chain.

Not covered here: aspect in the built-app journey (the picture-fit contract test covers it), audible output, native macOS or Windows, Bluetooth or surround devices, acoustic route latency against Chromium's own path, and receivers. Sound earlier than the picture is not offered: an element plays its sound and picture on one clock; earlier sound needs a separately buffered video presentation path and is tracked in [Let viewers play sound earlier than the picture](https://github.com/orgs/Mr-Streamer-OSS/projects/1?pane=issue&itemId=302242842), a 0.0.24 Planned follow-up blocked by #859. Delay only is the dispatcher’s accepted slice for #859, not a scope decision attributed to Wout.

Round-one fixes add strict synthetic assertions for delay (within 1 ms, including compressor lookahead), no full-scale samples for the measured program, at least 5 dB leveling, six channels retained and centre-only 5.1 input downmixed equally to stereo with leveling on/off (all onset checks allow 1 ms), offline render speed above 10 times realtime, and per-stage render capacity below 50%. The passing journey above recorded those measurements on this chain. They are synthetic qualification limits, not audible guarantees. No audible or native-platform proof is claimed.
