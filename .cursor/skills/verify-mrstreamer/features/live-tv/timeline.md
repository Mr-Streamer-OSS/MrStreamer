## Local retained timeline service

The local MPEG-TS route exposes `playback.timeline` and `playback.seekLive`, with arrival-time bounds, a separate cursor and expiry reporting. Its view owner retains capture while the renderer cancels/reloads a reader; `playback.close` releases that owner while recording readers continue. Thirty minutes is the default target, at most sixty through capture configuration, within the existing shared temporary-file budget. Local codec-repair conversion of clear muxed MPEG-TS HLS retains its output TS, including its captured sound selection. Selecting another converted-HLS audio track opens a new capture and timeline. Direct HLS passthrough, archives and receivers do not expose this timeline. The HLS resource cache is not a total memory ceiling.

Focused source proof: `apps/desktop/test/playback.test.ts` covers retained-reader cancellation, seek/Go live, recording lifetime, cleanup and time expiry below the cap. `apps/desktop/test/renderer/live-rewind.test.ts` covers local pause and same-session reload without a second open, and Watch's timeline (`apps/desktop/src/renderer/src/features/watch/WatchScreen.tsx`): the Live rewind group, slider value text, kept time, K pause, polling while paused, the expired-moment status and Go live, and that a stream without a timeline shows none and answers its keys with "This stream can't rewind".

Watch UI path: play a fake-provider MPEG-TS channel; the Live rewind group sits under the Watch controls with Pause, Back and Forward 10 seconds, the Position slider between the oldest kept and newest clock times, Live or "behind live", the kept time and Go live, disabled at live. Keys: Space or K pause, J and L skip, End goes live; the focused slider takes Left, Right, Page Up, Page Down, Home and End and seeks when let go. `pnpm verify:desktop live-tv` shows the group and its controls in `playing.ax.json` and `playing.png`, but does not drive them; `live-rewind` below does. CDP's accessibility tree reports a range input's native value as `valuetext`, so a slider's spoken text needs a screen reader check. The source tests additionally decode historical color and PID identity at 128 kbit/s and 2 Mbit/s, resolve intra-piece seeks, and protect sole-pin cleanup, slider commits, signed skips, bounded JPEG frozen-frame retention and clearing on resume/stop, pause during pending starts, converted-HLS audio reopening and OS media actions with a local retained carrier. Pending seeks reattach at the oldest retained byte if their original byte expired; active readers still report FellBehind. They do not qualify arbitrary historical tracks, native resources or thirty-minute runtime endurance.

Run `pnpm verify:desktop live-rewind` for the focused retained-timeline journey in `scripts/live-rewind.ts`. It uses the existing isolated launch, live doctor, fake-provider login and cleanup. It drives K pause/resume, J/L, slider Home, Go live, 720-pixel capture, then Record and closes Watch through Recordings while bytes continue. Its nonlooping one-hour fixture encodes twelve-bit seconds and asserts decoded markers against the fixture epoch and retained bounds for resume, J/L and slider cursor targets, and records provider requests, temporary-file bytes and Linux main-process RSS before/after pause and seeks. RSS excludes the renderer, GPU and converter; this is no total-app memory or performance ceiling. Quit must empty the owned history. `REWIND_EXPIRY=1` additionally waits for production thirty-minute expiry before resuming. A short run explicitly records expiry as unrun. `REWIND_RECOVERY=1` ends the owned fixture stream, waits for the replacement timeline before reading its slider, then requires a newer capture start and decoded new identity before checking recording continuity and cleanup. Historical track epochs, spoken accessibility, 12- and 24-hour clocks, all translated layouts and native resource/endurance qualification remain separate acceptance. The focused public Playback HTTP test in `apps/desktop/test/playback.test.ts` additionally cancels a converted-HLS decoder, observes capture advancing and decodes a historical replay frame.
