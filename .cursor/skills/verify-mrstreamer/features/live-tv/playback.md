# Live playback, failures and receivers

Live HLS waits for hls.js's pending capability checks, then starts with the lowest declared bitrate in its chosen codec/range tier and the same audio groups that still permits ABR to climb back to its initial level. It then adapts using measured throughput. An unresolved or rejected capability result is not used for a low-start override; Stop releases pending startup. `renderer/hls-startup.test.ts` covers audio-group preservation, delayed unsupported-level removal, cancellation and rejected queries. This stays within the selected provider feed: global/channel feed choices are unchanged, and catch-up retains its original start policy. Verify with a real multivariant fixture under constrained delivery and healthy delivery, observing initial segment requests, decoded width and clock progress; confirm healthy ABR can climb. Single-rendition/direct feeds, manual provider choices and catch-up still need their existing paths.

Direct HLS fatal failures select the exact loopback request before engine teardown. Converted MPEG-TS delivery from an HLS source uses the session-wide typed cause, including a later provider refusal or converter failure. Failed converted reconnect opens retain the existing 1, 2, 4 and 8 second schedule; the initial repair still has one try. A recovered segment, unrelated successful playlist, old request completion, subtitle failure or another session must not replace that cause. Parser/MSE errors select no HTTP cause; malformed and empty playlists take media recovery even though hls.js calls them network errors. `hls-failure.test.ts` and `renderer/hls-context.test.ts` cover request identity, attempt ordering and classification. Use real hls.js/MSE fault traces for native event ordering; passing stand-ins alone do not establish it. Preserve receiver behavior, provider admission and incident deduplication.

A failed channel's message, here or on a TV and in the mini player, closes with its cross (**Close message**). The channel stays failed and the message stays closed while the controls wake; **Watch** or R tries again and the next failure shows its own message. A lost TV's closed message stays closed while receivers are found or the list scans; reaching the TV again and losing it shows it again. R on a lost TV that has gone from the list is refused before it connects, so the TV stays lost and "… didn't answer" shows at the top right for a few seconds, each retry its own and closed with its own cross. Closed from the keyboard, focus moves to Watch, and Enter or Space on the cross is its own rather than the channel list's or a retry's. A TV connect that reached no receiver closes the same way, though its note sits outside the picture area. Renderer cases are in `renderer/live-recovery.test.ts` ("a failure's message") and `renderer/receiver.test.ts`.

For subtitle availability and subtitle-only outages, use `apps/desktop/test/e2e/live-subtitle-errors-app.ts`. Select cases with `MR_STREAMER_SUBTITLE_ERRORS_ONLY`; use `live-six-off` and `live-six-remembered` for discovery, and `live-segment-outage` to observe subtitle expiry followed by a video reconnect. For timing and look, open CC on `TEST | Subtitles and two sound tracks`, pick the teletext track, reopen CC and press Later, Earlier and Reset, then a Size, Background and Position: the value beside Timing follows, the menu stays open, and Escape hands G and H back. `renderer/live-more.test.ts` covers the rows, their show rules and the greyed rows while a TV plays. Require actual subtitle cues before the CC control appears, advancing picture during subtitle failure, restoration of the chosen language on reconnect and explicit Off remaining off. The fixture remains synthetic; actual-provider and native-platform acceptance stay separate.

Automatic Cast heartbeat replies that exceed the frame limit must close the channel and use its normal reconnect path without an uncaught main-process error. If stopping a dropped load exceeds the frame limit, stop the owned app through bounded disconnect cleanup and report the session lost instead of reconnecting with the same oversized identifier. Without user disconnect, the TLS fixture must observe abandoned media/app cleared when app STOP fits; if app STOP cannot fit or is unanswered, require closed sockets, one lost event, rejected later commands and a successful new connection/load. A receiver that cannot be commanded may still play; no remote-stop guarantee applies there. Disconnect must still attempt app STOP independently when media STOP is too large, settle and close, as must failed-setup cleanup. Late dropped-load status during explicit disconnect must not emit a loss. `apps/desktop/test/cast.test.ts` uses an owned TLS receiver; existing normal cancellation and recovery cases must preserve genuine current loads. Explicit oversized requests still fail immediately with the typed request-too-large result. This proves the adapter contract, not a hardware receiver or a packaged Electron process.
