# Catalogue, metadata and freshness

The catalogue worker initializes the same pinned fetch and matching classes as main before creating provider and TMDB clients; threads have independent globals. Check ordinary provider refresh, metadata progress, cancellation and restart with `ondemand.test.ts`, `metadata.test.ts`, `catalogue-persistence.test.ts` and `packages/core/test/metadata-lifecycle.test.ts`, then drive the built app's `titles` and `language` journeys. These compatibility checks do not prove behavior for malformed network responses or resolve connection-level advisories.

Opened provider details persist per saved source and accepted catalogue, with 24-hour freshness. TMDB descriptions and seasons persist per ID/language, refresh at 150 days, expire at 182 days and retry missing answers after one day. On an offline same-account restart, previously opened movie/series/season details remain readable with zero detail requests while valid. Refresh, changed credentials/import settings and removed titles must not restore fresh evidence for an old file. Failed renewal may retain listed display data; stale episodes cannot authorize playback, downloads or subtitle matching. `ondemand-details.test.ts` and `detail-cache.test.ts` cover restart, concurrency/cancellation, removal, clock correction, bounds and individual corrupt-record misses. Drive titles/downloads on the built app and inspect requests and retained screenshots separately. Explicit viewing-data erasure also securely removes shared title/season detail keys and timestamps, which otherwise reveal opened-title history. Ordinary source forgetting retains those shared descriptions.

Catalogue metadata belongs to the shared core store. `packages/core/src/metadata/policy.ts` owns its 150-day refresh and 182-day expiry durations, also used by desktop details and iOS titles/details. Caller-specific cutoff and clock checks remain local; iOS provider records keep their separate age limit. `apps/ios/test/saved-details.test.ts` checks exact refresh, expiry and clock-drift boundaries through saved details and public cache reads. Changing the ordered wanted titles or language aborts obsolete title/service requests and pacing/retry waits. Equal-content wants in the same language preserve running requests and recheck missing answers afterwards, including a refresh during offline retry completion. After title requests give up, the pass skips the weekly service crawl so a pending refresh retries titles first. An equal refresh during a healthy crawl waits for it to finish. Equal demand after an offline give-up can also restart fetching. Late success, missing and refusal answers cannot publish into the replacement run. Completed answers remain reusable by TMDB ID and their actual language across temporary narrower wants, adult visibility changes, source removal and restart. Fetch demand removes no cached answer; the existing 150-day refresh and 182-day hard expiry still apply. A cancelled English fallback resumes without fetching the completed primary answer again. Worker shutdown terminally closes the store and awaits requests and queued cache writes within the existing worker stop deadline. Details reads retain the actual adult setting when reading catalogue stamps. `packages/core/test/metadata-lifecycle.test.ts` covers cancellation, equal demand during fetching, during offline retry completion and after offline give-up, narrow/empty wants with persistence/restart and a 25,000-title warm wanted set. `apps/desktop/test/metadata.test.ts` covers the main service's adult policy and existing notification timing. The separately bounded opened-detail cache keeps its existing policy. Desktop `titles` and `language` proof supplements these contracts; native iOS behavior requires its own run.

Filtered page search counts every match while retaining only its best bounded page. Sparse matches beyond the first page remain findable, and the selected version must satisfy all file filters together. Prepared aliases always consult current metadata before reuse; expiry, missing answers, language changes and edited names must remove old spellings. `title-filters.test.ts` and the public core search corpus preserve totals, companion versions, exact-name ranking and account ownership. Measure first-process indexing separately from warm scans and query-cache hits.

Catalogue updates during a first read must show current titles in Home, For you and All movies, even when the old IPC answer arrives after `ondemand.updated`. Cached posters stay visible during refresh. `renderer/ondemand-first-reads.test.ts` covers those rendered paths with held IPC answers; `renderer/episode-rows.test.ts` covers open details and seasons keeping their answers through the same event. The titles baseline does not force this timing. For built-app race proof, use the isolated Electron/CDP harness to hold the first list response at the IPC boundary, send the update event, release the old answer and inspect the resulting posters and read count.

Related rows in details are the exception, because only the lists mark a title for adults. When `ondemand.updated` names lists fetched anew (a changed `fetchedAt` or subscription), or carries `markedForAdults` (two accepted lists can share a `fetchedAt`), every related row read before them goes: an open row shows its loading line until its new answer, a closed one reads again as it opens, and a late answer from the earlier lists is dropped. TMDB metadata alone keeps the picks on screen while they are read again. `renderer/related.test.ts` holds those answers. The titles baseline marks nothing for adults; for built-app proof, add a second provider, open A, related B and related C, have that fixture mark B for adults, refresh it in Settings, then watch C's row on return and A's row reopened from its poster with a DOM observer.

Lists of titles to browse keep their posters through an ordinary refresh, but not after one whose lists mark for adults a title they listed before. The catalogue worker compares the row the lists show for each id, the first, with the one before it, by the provider's flag and adult categories, and `ondemand.updated` then carries `markedForAdults`; a later duplicate row changes nothing. Home's recommendations, rows, collections, page and palette searches, Continue watching's title lookups and Watchlist pages then drop what they kept, while open details keep theirs: an open one shows its loading state until its new answer, a closed one reads again as it opens, and a late answer from before is dropped. `ondemand.test.ts` covers the flag and `renderer/adult-visibility.test.ts` those views; rows follow the same rule without a case of their own. In the built app, also save B, play A's first episode to its end so Home recommends titles like it, and search for B in Series and the palette, then refresh Backup with only Movies showing and visit each view with a DOM observer.
