# iOS autoplay, speed and receivers

On iOS, an episode that plays to its end in Watch counts down ten seconds in the panel's text-button row, "S1 E3 · Name Plays in N" with **Cancel** and **Play next**, to the next unwatched episode the viewing record identifies, or the next episode in order when none is unwatched, in the same version. It resumes the selected file's own progress with watch marks respected; a watched episode starts at zero, and the last episode has no countdown. Pause, Space, a seek, Close, Stop, Picture in Picture, another screen or request, and a replaced provider login end it, as do leaving the app (system overlays such as Control Center count) and hiding the panel; a refused save means none starts. Cancel returns the row with manual **Next episode**. A downloaded copy counts down the same way, offline too, but only to its saved immediate successor while that exact copy is complete, allowed, on the phone and still the recorded byte size, and offers **Next episode** only while it is; one that isn't, or goes during the countdown, ends it with the copy kept at its end, and nothing streams in its place. Entry points: `apps/ios/src/playback.ts` (`countDown`, `useUpNext`, `playUpNext`, `cancelUpNext`), `apps/ios/src/play-title.ts` (`upNext`), `apps/ios/src/downloads.ts` (`playCopy`'s `upNext`) and `UpNextRow` in `apps/ios/src/watch.tsx`. In `downloads-providers.test.ts`'s offline Next cases, one plays a copy to its end and on with no provider or network; others remove, lose, truncate, download again or exclude the next copy, or remove the ended one, around its countdown, and one completes the next copy during the episode. `playback-requests.test.ts` drives the real episode plays through each ending and the last episode; `playback.test.ts` drives the row, Cancel, Play next, Space, Escape, leaving the app and hiding the panel. A viewer-held Pause blocks an end from starting a countdown. An end arriving within two seconds of a seek short of the end is treated as obsolete, including across Pause and Play; a later skip or scrub to the end supersedes that seek and can count down. A genuine end within that short window can also be suppressed, leaving manual Next episode; Next episode pressed just before the end still plays the next at once; leaving the app also drops a countdown still asking which episode goes on. Without a native slot, AVPlayer's own end event, VoiceOver focus, order and its single announcement, and the row at large text remain unproven.

On iOS, **Watch > Speed** offers 0.5×, 0.75×, 1×, 1.25×, 1.5× and 2× for movies and episodes, saving the choice for later titles. Its label and checkmark follow the player's reported rate; changing speed while paused leaves playback paused. Live and catch-up use 1× and offer no Speed control. `apps/ios/src/watch.tsx` (`TitleSpeed`), `apps/ios/src/playback.ts` (`setSpeed`, `playbackSpeed`) and `apps/ios/src/media-preferences.ts` own the choice and readback. `apps/ios/src/media-controls.ts` (`startMediaControls`) and `apps/ios/modules/media-controls/` publish Now Playing while Watch, Picture in Picture, AirPlay or a Watch set aside owns the player, never for Home's silent preview. Titles publish their name, line, position, rate and length when known. Live and catch-up publish playback rates too; their content identity is limited to the channel name and Live, or the programme name and "Catch-up", with no provider, login, address or artwork. Remote play/pause/skip/seek use the current player's intents; next is available only in Watch, play or pause cancels a countdown, stale item commands do nothing, and Stop or failure clear metadata and commands. Close clears them when neither AirPlay nor Picture in Picture retains the player; while either remains, its metadata and supported commands remain. `apps/ios/test/playback.test.ts` and `media-preferences.test.ts` cover portable behavior with native stand-ins. Native proof requires an arm64 app build, a real title's speed/readback and paused speed change, matching Control Center metadata and commands, clearing after Close with no retained AirPlay or Picture in Picture session, and retaining publication while either session continues. Compilation, simulator observations, physical remote buttons, background playback, Picture in Picture and AirPlay must be recorded separately; stand-ins and module discovery do not establish native acceptance.

On iOS, Watch's TV button (**Play on TV**) plays a movie or episode on a Cast TV through the phone's relay (`apps/ios/src/cast.ts`, `cast-media.ts`, `apps/ios/modules/cast`). Matroska goes as fMP4 HLS from a format session opened at 0 with resume in the LOAD's `currentTime`, refused before the TV gets anything when its playlist names a codec outside H.264 up to 4.1, 1080p and AAC, or names none (HEVC); MP4/MOV go byte-range proxied with every track and are not checked first, so a TV that can't play one says so after the LOAD. AVI is refused. Audio, Subtitles and Speed are hidden while a TV plays. **Disconnect and play here** plays the title here from the TV's position, paused. `apps/ios/test/cast.test.ts` and `cast-media.test.ts` prove the sender against a fake TV and the rewriting on Linux; the module builds for the iOS 27 simulator, where Watch shows the TV button and chooser; Local Network on a phone, a real receiver, the native relay's network behavior, playback and background timing need a device and are unproven.
