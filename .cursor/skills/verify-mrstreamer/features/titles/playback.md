# Title playback, recovery and receivers

From desktop 0.0.16 the bundled tools decode VP8, VP9 and AV1 for title and offline conversion. `apps/desktop/scripts/verify-ffmpeg.ts <target>` checks the shipped features and every frame of the committed VP8 WebM, 10-bit VP9 MP4/WebM and 10-bit AV1 MKV/MP4 clips before and after H.264 conversion. A source build using system FFmpeg is not this proof. `apps/desktop/test/e2e/installed-media.ts` checks native installed AV1 controls, downloaded copies and actual copied-video recovery with bundled tools; Mac also runs the HEVC 12-bit and H.264 10-bit fixtures. Require per-window delivery headers, decoded picture/audio progress and no served provider reads after fixture shutdown; an unavailable required rejection branch remains unresolved. Native acceptance needs an actual run. Conversion outputs 8-bit SDR, without an HDR claim.

On-demand reads hold while a paused picture is available, allowing a pending read to finish. A paused seek or loss of the current picture can read until its picture decodes; Play resumes the same run. A pause below the full-buffer threshold releases reads on Play; a wait entered at 60 seconds still drains below 40 seconds before reading again. Run `xvfb-run -a node apps/desktop/test/e2e/title-compatibility.ts paused-buffer` after building. This uses the unchanged long-title fixture with constrained renderer delivery to check held appends, paused seek, resume, close and offline playback; it does not measure native memory or provider-wide savings.

Title delivery failures retry four times at 1/2/4/8 seconds. Brief returns, pauses, buffering and seeks do not restore the budget; 30 seconds of observed playback progress does. A new title or explicit Retry restores it. Local parser/MSE failures use media recovery, fetch/body failures use network recovery, and native MediaError code 2 is network. Preserve pause, position, tracks and one logical play through retries. `renderer/title-engine.test.ts` and `title-recovery.test.ts` cover the engine/controller contracts; native decoding/audio and real-provider acceptance remain separate.

Copied-video rejection in a local movie, episode or offline copy first tries useful sound conversion when the failing track is unknown, then H.264 video conversion once. A known rejected video codec can skip sound conversion. Startup and rejection after playback use the same finite session budget; paused recovery, seeks and track changes keep position, tracks and conversion. Network and typed upstream/tool failures do not spend that budget. `title-recovery.test.ts` and `title-engine.test.ts` exercise those controller/engine contracts, while `titles.test.ts` and `downloads.test.ts` serve high-depth copies and verify sticky conversion, clocks and one provider reader or zero offline requests. The synthetic `title-h264-10bit.mkv` and `title-hevc-12bit.mkv` derive from the existing two-track subtitle fixture using libx264/yuv420p10le and libx265/yuv420p12le, with stereo AAC and copied subtitles. Ordinary supported video still copies; receivers keep their existing H.264/AAC plans and cuts. Conversion retains existing SDR yuv420p behavior, without an HDR promise. Run `xvfb-run -a node apps/desktop/test/e2e/title-compatibility.ts <variant>` after building, with `MR_STREAMER_FFMPEG=/usr/bin/ffmpeg` for explicit Linux system-tool proof. Variants are `h264-10bit`, `hevc-12bit`, `h264-rejected-copy`, `h264-later-rejection` and `paused-buffer` (the paused-read scenario above). The first two establish actual platform behavior. The rejected-copy variant keeps valid High 10 pictures but changes the AVC configuration's profile declaration to an invalid value, provoking real MSE type rejection; the later-rejection variant appends an impossible ISO box through the actual SourceBuffer after confirmed playback, provoking real browser MediaError code 3 twice to cover sound then video recovery while paused. Neither substitutes a fake engine or media error. Each drives Movies and Watch offline with fresh owned profiles, captures delivery headers, Chromium media diagnostics, decoded progress, pause/seek/audio/mini/fullscreen and one-reader/offline-request facts, retains screenshots/proof under `.local/verification/`, then tears down. Built-app rejection proof must record real MSE/decode refusal, actual response delivery and decoded frame/audio progress; service decoding alone does not prove browser playback. Native bundled and installed acceptance remains separate from Linux source-build proof.

A failed movie or episode, here or on a TV, says why in the middle of the picture, or in Next Up for a next episode that didn't start. Its cross (**Close message**, top right of the message) hides that message only: the phase stays failed, nothing claims the TV plays, the controls return and **Play** or Space tries again, and the next failure shows its own message. A lost TV's closed message stays closed while receivers are found or the list scans; reaching the TV again and losing it shows it again. **Play** on a lost TV that has gone from the list is refused before it connects, so the TV stays lost and "… didn't answer" shows at the top right for a few seconds, each retry its own and closed with its own cross. Tab to the cross and Enter or Space closes it without starting anything, and focus moves to Play. The mini player's failure word and "Subtitles unavailable" close the same way. Renderer cases are in `next-episode.test.ts`, `receiver.test.ts` and `title-subtitles.test.ts`; in the built app, fail an open with the fake provider, close the message, require the controls and a retry request.

For movie playback, seeking, release of provider connections and subtitles use `apps/desktop/test/e2e/packaged-app.ts`. That installed-package smoke also replaces a previously unopened MP4 movie with a bounded runtime-generated VP9/AAC MP4 from committed fixtures, then opens it through Movies and requires decoded picture/audio and a ten-second seek. The provider changes the actual bytes and file identity; no probe or capability state is seeded. For episode menus, numbering and watched-state behavior use `episode-marks.ts`. Inspect `TitlesPage.tsx` and the current filter labels before driving Quality/Language, and compare the visible matching poster/count and selected version. Filtering on Verified requires a file already observed by real playback; do not seed observations by writing internal state.

A press on the Position scrubber leaves the keys with the player: the thumb gives focus back when the pointer lets go, or as its focus arrives when a quick click ended first. Focus that went to another control during the press stays there, and a thumb reached with Tab keeps its focus and its arrow keys. A finger is the same: its press is left to Base UI's touch handling, which focuses the thumb at once, so a tap or a drag also hands the keys back when it lifts. The rule is `SliderControl` in `components/ui/slider.tsx`, which the Volume slider shares. Its renderer cases are in `renderer/title-scrubber-keys.test.ts`; they stand in for layout, send untrusted events and cover Position only, by mouse and by a finger's tap and drag. In the built app, click Position once with no dwell during movie playback, then require that Space pauses and resumes, that ArrowRight skips ten seconds and that Escape leaves the title. Then Tab to Position and require that ArrowRight moves it one second with the focus ring still on the thumb. Repeat the click on Volume. For touch, send a tap and a drag on Position with CDP `Input.dispatchTouchEvent`, which delivers both the pointer and the touch events, and require the new position and the same keys. That is not a touchscreen: hardware touch stays unverified until someone uses one.

Local and receiver title opens share `PlaybackCoordination` in `services/playback-coordination.ts` for serialized admission, latest-turn checks and download reclaim. Playback retains file probing, serving and its per-title upstream reader policy. Downloaded copies register without a subscription, use no provider lease and preserve receiver sessions.

A playlist film's headers go whole only to its file's origin: after a redirect elsewhere, a Referer holding the link's token goes cut to its origin, for playback, a receiver and downloads alike. The file's own token-named query values don't follow an https redirect to http where the login rule recognizes them, unlike a Live stream's; its other query values count only as whole parts. `playlist-credentials.test.ts` checks probing and a download transfer against a loopback http server; `playlist-import.test.ts` keeps the exact-file and Live redirect policies.

A provider that ignores byte ranges can resume and skip by reading its whole answer forward to the requested byte position. Verify both local playback and a receiver segment away from zero against ranged playback, including decoded pictures, start timestamps, bytes sent and one provider connection. `titles.test.ts` and `receiver.test.ts` cover those service paths. Such a provider is never asked for a subtitle track's past: the lines before a position come only from what a run from the start kept of the same file, so after a whole run a skip shows the line on screen with no provider request, while before one, after a replaced file, or with answers that carry no strong validator (a strong ETag, or a Last-Modified at least a second older than the answer) or don't say how long the file is, it is `unreadable`. Keyframe-index reads still refuse it. Correct 206 answers whose headers do not establish the range still pass through as before; verify their exact bytes. Local startup waits while discarded prefix bytes advance, then gives the picture a full 30 seconds after the final byte. A drop stalled for 30 seconds fails as before; leaving aborts the wait immediately. Main sends `playback.readingAhead` at most once every 3 seconds only as dropping advances. The starting title engine follows matching session notices for its existing 45-second no-picture and 30-second unpaused-start watchdogs. With no notices it fails as before, and ranged providers send none. Verify an advancing slowed far resume lasting more than 30 seconds through a moving picture, plus a stalled answer that fails. Service cases in `titles.test.ts` use an injected startup deadline; `renderer/title-reading-ahead.test.ts` uses the real player and engine with a held response and browser media stand-ins. Receivers retain the 30-second segment deadline, so a large or slow far segment can return 504 and a retry can read from zero again.

Seek step, typed jumps and the sleep timer (0.0.24, Paper A8). **Settings > Playback > Seek step** (`apps/desktop/src/renderer/src/features/settings/GeneralSection.tsx`) saves `seekStepSeconds` from `SEEK_STEPS`, listing a saved step outside them as it is; title keys, both skip-button sets and Live TV rewind move by it, as do title system media keys when the system supplies no offset, through `seekStep`/`useSeekStep` in `apps/desktop/src/renderer/src/lib/preferences.ts`, and the buttons name it. The time before the title's scrubber (`GoToTime` in `TitleWatch.tsx`) is a button that opens a field; Enter sends `parseClock`'s seconds to `titlePlayer.seek`, which uses the scrubber's seek: it clamps to the length, keeps buffered seeks in place and snaps into a growing recording's verified ranges when starting a new run; a time it can't read, including a total outside safe integer seconds, stays marked with an example; Escape or blur puts the time back. While the field is open it counts as the title's open menu, so the controls stay and the player keys wait. Removing the field clears that menu state, including at the episode end screen, so Escape can leave. `SleepMenu.tsx`, after Speed, calls `titlePlayer.setSleep` with 1, 15, 30, 45, 60 or 90 minutes, the end of this episode or title, or Off; the button is pressed and names the clock time it pauses at, or End, and its note names the receiver when one plays. The title player owns the timer (see `title-player.ts`). `apps/desktop/test/renderer/sleep-timer.test.ts` covers firing, next episode, pause, close, profile switch, the end, held receiver open/load replies, rejected receiver pause with retry advice, and expired intent on title replacement, failed startup and receiver handoff; `copy-next.test.ts` the end with a copy; `apps/desktop/test/renderer/title-controls.test.ts` the step on keys and buttons, the field and the menu; `live-rewind.test.ts` the rewind step. `pnpm verify:desktop title-controls` (source `scripts/title-controls.ts`) saves a 30-second step through Settings, checks the named skip buttons and a 30-second Right on a decoded fake-provider movie within five seconds, bounded on both sides with elapsed playback allowed, jumps to 0:05 through the field, and sets the one-minute timer, requiring the movie paused no sooner than a minute later with the note shown and the button off. It runs on Linux with Xvfb; receiver pausing, the end timer and recordings have source proof only, including controller tests for receiver expiry and rejection; no physical receiver run is claimed.
